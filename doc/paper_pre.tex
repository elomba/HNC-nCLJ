\title{Component Diversity, Excess Entropy, and Divalent-Ion Self-Diffusion in Charged Lennard-Jones Mixtures}

\author{E. Lomba}
\email{e.lomba@csic.es}
\affiliation{Instituto de Qu{\'\i}mica F{\'\i}sica Blas Cabrera, CSIC, Serrano 119, E-28006 Madrid, Spain}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Does increasing the number of mixture components raise excess entropy and accelerate divalent ions? We combine HNC screening of 35 candidates with replicated molecular dynamics of eight selected charged Lennard-Jones mixtures at $T^*=2$, $\rho^*=0.7$. Each mixture contains unchanged $+2/-2$ probe fractions of 0.03 per sign, with $N=4000$ and three independent replicas. Excess entropy is obtained from 19-density thermodynamic integration with a fixed shifted LJ cutoff and a screened dilute endpoint; transport uses one common center-of-mass frame. High-entropy solvent designs raise MD excess entropy by approximately $0.30k_B$ per particle and divalent self-diffusion by 18--20\% relative to the original baselines. A more controlled diversification, preserving charged composition and mean bare LJ pair amplitude, raises excess entropy by $0.103k_B$, mean self-diffusion by 8--9\%, and probe diffusion by approximately 5\%. The small ion effect is sensitive to MSD fitting window and unresolved with monovalent background. An exact label-only split changes formal ideal mixing entropy but leaves diffusion identical; adding monovalent salt alone gives no resolved probe enhancement. Both mean and probe diffusion show positive descriptive Rosenfeld-like trends, with weaker probe slopes. Residual charge-sign asymmetry and finite-size uncertainty limit quantitative claims. The results identify faster-ion, higher-excess-entropy mixtures, but establish neither a causal component-count effect nor a universal species scaling law.
A corrected classical 2PT analysis passes internal and label checks but retains composition-dependent entropy biases, supporting spectral diagnostics while leaving thermodynamic integration as the primary entropy estimate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Excess-entropy scaling offers a link between equilibrium correlations and liquid transport. Rosenfeld proposed that an appropriately reduced diffusion coefficient obeys~\cite{Rosenfeld1977,Rosenfeld1999}
\begin{equation}
D_i^R=A_i\exp(B_i s_{\rm ex}/k_B),\qquad
D_i^R=D_i\rho^{1/3}\sqrt{m_i/(k_BT)}.
\label{eq:rosenfeld_general}
\end{equation}
For mixtures, whether this relation describes individual charged species as well as the solvent-dominated mean is a separate question. Local solvation, association, and interaction-dependent friction can respond differently to composition. Generalized tracer scaling includes additional kinetic information and is not guaranteed when component mobilities decouple~\cite{Krekelberg2009}.

The entropy entering Eq.~\eqref{eq:rosenfeld_general} is the excess per particle relative to the ideal mixture at the same temperature, density, and composition. The ideal reference already includes $s_{\rm mix}^{\rm id}/k_B=-\sum_i x_i\ln x_i$. A greater number of species can therefore increase total entropy without increasing $s_{\rm ex}$ or changing any force. Distinguishing label multiplicity from physical interaction diversity is essential to testing a component-induced speedup.

Our earlier four-mixture comparison identified an inconsistent LJ cutoff along the density-integration path and incompatible archived ion-transport estimates. The fixed-cutoff correction was material for absolute entropy but could not resolve species mobility. Here we replace the earlier transport evidence with a larger, independently replicated HNC-guided study, including an exact label-only null and physically diverse solvents at matched mean bare interaction amplitude. The original cutoff audit is retained in Appendix~\ref{app:cutoff}; its four-mixture transport and 2PT decompositions are not used in the present scaling analysis.

We distinguish the particle-averaged self-diffusion $D_{\rm net}=\sum_i x_iD_i$ from mutual diffusion and conductivity. The divalent quantity is the average self-diffusion of the two probe signs, not motion of neutral ion pairs. The central tests are whether physical diversification raises MD excess entropy, whether the probes accelerate reproducibly, and whether their response is as strong as that of the mean fluid.

\section{Model and Theoretical Framework}

\subsection{Interaction Potentials and Mixture Specifications}

We consider a classical fluid mixture of $p$ components characterized by number densities $\rho_i = N_i / V$ ($i = 1, \dots, p$), with total number density $\rho = \sum_{i=1}^p \rho_i$ and mole fractions $x_i = \rho_i / \rho$. The particles interact via a cut-and-shifted Lennard-Jones (LJ) core augmented by Coulomb interactions:
\begin{equation}
u_{ij}(r) = u_{ij}^{\text{LJ}}(r) + \frac{q_i q_j e^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r r},
\label{eq:total_potential}
\end{equation}
where the short-range LJ contribution is truncated and shifted at $r_c = 2.5\,\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
u_{ij}^{\text{LJ}}(r) = 
\begin{cases}
4\varepsilon_{ij} \left[ \left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^6 \right] - u_{ij}^{\text{shift}}, & r \le r_c, \\
0, & r > r_c.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
All particles share identical diameters $\sigma_{ij} = \sigma = 1.0$ and masses $m_i = m = 1.0$. Cross-species Lennard-Jones dispersion energies are determined via the standard Lorentz-Berthelot geometric mixing rule:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{ij} = \sqrt{\varepsilon_i \varepsilon_j}.
\end{equation}
Reduced Lennard-Jones units are employed throughout: length $r^* = r/\sigma$, density $\rho^* = \rho \sigma^3$, temperature $T^* = k_B T / \varepsilon_1$, energy $u^* = u/\varepsilon_1$, and time $t^* = t / \tau$ with $\tau = \sigma \sqrt{m/\varepsilon_1}$.

The reduced Coulomb energy prefactor is $\Gamma=e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\sigma\varepsilon_1)=1.0$. The dimensionless Bjerrum length is therefore $\lambda_B^*=e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\sigma k_BT)=\Gamma/T^*=0.50$ at $T^*=2.00$. The parameter $\Gamma$ used in the input is an energy prefactor, not the Bjerrum length itself. The ionic strength of the mixture is given by
\begin{equation}
I = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^p x_i q_i^2.
\label{eq:ionic_strength}
\end{equation}
The Debye screening parameter $\kappa$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
\kappa = \sqrt{4\pi \beta^* \rho^* \sum_{i=1}^p x_i q_i^2} = \sqrt{8\pi \beta^* \rho^* I}.
\label{eq:debye_kappa}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ornstein-Zernike Equations and HNC Closure}

The microscopic structure of the multicomponent fluid is described by the matrix of total correlation functions $h_{ij}(r) = g_{ij}(r) - 1$ and direct correlation functions $c_{ij}(r)$, coupled via the multicomponent Ornstein-Zernike (OZ) relations~\cite{HansenMcDonald4th}:
\begin{equation}
h_{ij}(r) = c_{ij}(r) + \sum_{k=1}^p \rho_k \int c_{ik}(|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|) h_{kj}(r') \, d\mathbf{r}'.
\label{eq:oz_relation}
\end{equation}
In Fourier space, defining $\tilde{h}_{ij}(k)$ and $\tilde{c}_{ij}(k)$ via standard three-dimensional Fourier transforms, Eq.~\eqref{eq:oz_relation} takes the matrix form
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k) = \tilde{\mathbf{C}}(k) + \tilde{\mathbf{C}}(k) \mathbf{P} \tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k),
\label{eq:oz_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{P}_{ij} = \rho_i \delta_{ij}$ is the diagonal density matrix.

To treat the long-range Coulomb tail without numerical truncation artifacts, we employ the Allnatt screening decomposition~\cite{Allnatt1964,Lomba1994}. The pair potential is split into short-range (SR) and long-range (LR) parts:
\begin{equation}
u_{ij}(r) = u_{ij}^{\text{SR}}(r) + u_{ij}^{\text{LR}}(r),
\end{equation}
with $u_{ij}^{\text{LR}}(r) = q_i q_j \Gamma \psi(r)$, where $\psi(r) = \text{erf}(\alpha r) / r$ and $\alpha$ is an optimized Ewald splitting parameter. The direct correlation function is decomposed accordingly:
\begin{equation}
c_{ij}(r) = c_{ij}^{\text{SR}}(r) - \beta u_{ij}^{\text{LR}}(r).
\end{equation}
The Hypernetted Chain (HNC) closure expresses the short-range cavity function $\gamma_{ij}(r) = h_{ij}(r) - c_{ij}(r)$ as
\begin{equation}
g_{ij}(r) = \exp\left[ -\beta u_{ij}(r) + \gamma_{ij}(r) \right],
\label{eq:hnc_closure}
\end{equation}
which corresponds to setting the bridge function $B_{ij}(r) \equiv 0$. The resulting coupled non-linear integral equations are solved iteratively on a radial grid of $N_r = 4096$ points with grid spacing $\Delta r = 0.005\,\sigma$, using the multicomponent integral-equation solver~\cite{Lomba1996}.

\subsection{Thermodynamic Properties and Free Energy Functional}

Once the correlation functions $g_{ij}(r)$ and $c_{ij}(r)$ are obtained, the excess internal energy per particle is evaluated from the energy equation:
\begin{equation}
\frac{u_{\text{ex}}}{k_B T} = \frac{1}{2} \beta \rho \sum_{i,j=1}^p x_i x_j \int_0^\infty 4\pi r^2 u_{ij}(r) g_{ij}(r) \, dr.
\label{eq:energy_route}
\end{equation}
The compressibility factor $Z = P / (\rho k_B T)$ is obtained via the virial route:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\text{virial}} = 1 - \frac{\beta \rho}{6} \sum_{i,j=1}^p x_i x_j \int_0^\infty 4\pi r^3 \frac{du_{ij}(r)}{dr} g_{ij}(r) \, dr.
\label{eq:virial_route}
\end{equation}

Within the HNC approximation, the excess Helmholtz free energy per particle $a_{\text{ex}} = A_{\text{ex}} / (N k_B T)$ can be computed directly as a closed-form functional of the pair correlation functions without performing numerical parameter integrations~\cite{Morita1960,Singer1985,Lomba1994}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
a_{\text{ex}}^{\text{HNC}} = & -\frac{1}{2\rho} \sum_{i,j=1}^p \rho_i \rho_j \int d\mathbf{r} \left[ \frac{1}{2} h_{ij}^2(r) - c_{ij}(r) h_{ij}(r) \right. \\
& \left. \vphantom{\frac{1}{2}} + h_{ij}(r) - (h_{ij}(r) + 1) \ln(h_{ij}(r) + 1) \right] \\
& - \frac{1}{2\rho (2\pi)^3} \int d\mathbf{k} \left[ \ln \det(\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{P}^{1/2} \tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k) \mathbf{P}^{1/2}) \right. \\
& \left. \vphantom{\ln \det} - \text{Tr}\left(\mathbf{P} \tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k)\right) \right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:hnc_free_energy}
\end{equation}

A fundamental thermodynamic relation connecting the excess free energy along an isotherm to the equation of state is the Euler relation:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\text{diff}} = 1 + \rho \left( \frac{\partial a_{\text{ex}}}{\partial \rho} \right)_T.
\label{eq:z_diff}
\end{equation}
Conversely, integrating the equation of state from the ideal gas limit along the isotherm yields the excess free energy via thermodynamic integration:
\begin{equation}
a_{\text{ex}}(\rho) = \int_0^\rho \frac{Z(\rho') - 1}{\rho'} \, d\rho'.
\label{eq:ti_path}
\end{equation}
Numerical differentiation and virial-route agreement are internal HNC consistency tests, as discussed in the accompanying theory report. They do not establish agreement with MD, nor do they supply transport coefficients. The new HNC screening values and replicated MD comparisons are reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

The excess entropy per particle $s_{\text{ex}} / k_B$ then follows from the thermodynamic definition:
\begin{equation}
\frac{s_{\text{ex}}}{k_B} = \frac{u_{\text{ex}}}{k_B T} - a_{\text{ex}}.
\label{eq:sex_thermo}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Green-Wallace Pair Structural Entropy}

In addition to the total thermodynamic excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}}$, we compute the two-body structural entropy $s^{(2)}$ from the Green-Wallace expansion~\cite{Green1952,Wallace1987}. For a multicomponent mixture, $s^{(2)} / k_B$ is expressed as
\begin{equation}
\frac{s^{(2)}}{k_B} = -\frac{1}{2} \rho \sum_{i,j=1}^p x_i x_j \int \bigl[ g_{ij}(r) \ln g_{ij}(r) - (g_{ij}(r) - 1) \bigr] d\mathbf{r}.
\label{eq:s2_definition}
\end{equation}
The integrand in Eq.~\eqref{eq:s2_definition} vanishes identically as $g_{ij}(r) \to 1$ at large distances, ensuring rapid convergence. As shown by Wallace~\cite{Wallace1987}, $s^{(2)}$ represents the loss of translational entropy associated with static spatial pair correlations.

\section{Historical Cutoff Audit and Superseded Estimates}
\label{app:cutoff}

The earlier $N=2000$ comparison comprised a 94\% neutral-solvent 3C baseline, two 4C liquids containing 89\% primary solvent and 5\% secondary solvent with $\varepsilon=1.25$ or 0.75, and the 84\%-solvent 5C baseline with 5\% of each monovalent sign. All contained 3\% of each divalent sign. These 4C states differ from U4 and the new 30\%-secondary-solvent designs.

The original density sweep changed the LJ cutoff with density, so integrating $(Z-1)/\rho$ did not follow a single Hamiltonian. Repeating all 60 states with a common shifted LJ cutoff $2.5\sigma$ corrected that error. Retaining the original pressure estimator and finite-intercept quadrature isolates cutoff-only dense-state entropy shifts of $-0.08717$, $-0.08565$, $-0.08501$, and $-0.09107k_B$ for 3C, strongly attractive 4C, weakly attractive 4C, and 5C. The dense-state interactions and energies were already unchanged; the correction arose from lower-density pressure integration. Approximately half of the former HNC--MD entropy gap was removed.

With the historical target-temperature mean-pressure estimator and screened integration, the corrected $N=2000$ entropies were $-1.96693$, $-1.97261$, $-1.96199$, and $-1.97588k_B$. These values belong to the historical protocol: they do not use the new configurational pressure reference, particle number, density grid, or replica sampling. The new B3 and B5 values in Table~\ref{tab:new_states} supersede them for the present comparisons. The 4C values remain an auxiliary dispersion comparison and are not combined with the new transport estimates.

The archived species MSDs used separate group-COM subtraction and were not consistent with all separately reported VACF/2PT transport values. Those estimates and unverified species-entropy decompositions have therefore been removed from the quantitative transport argument. No physical charge-asymmetric caging mechanism or universal Rosenfeld exponent is inferred from them. The common-frame replicated trajectories now provide the primary evidence; their own remaining convergence limitations are reported explicitly.
