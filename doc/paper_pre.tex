\author{E. Lomba}

\begin{abstract}
We present a comprehensive investigation of the thermodynamics, excess entropy, and self-diffusion dynamics across multicomponent charged Lennard-Jones mixtures along a supercritical isotherm ($T^* = 2.00$, $\beta^* = 0.50$, $\Gamma = 1.0$) up to dense liquid packing ($\rho^* = 0.70$). We contrast four systematically varied fluids: (i) a 3-component symmetric electrolyte ($94\%$ solvent and $6\%$ 2:2 salt, ionic strength $I = 0.12$); (ii) a 4-component fluid featuring an enhanced solvent dispersion energy ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25\,\varepsilon$, $5\%$ mole fraction); (iii) a 4-component fluid featuring a weakened solvent dispersion energy ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75\,\varepsilon$, $5\%$ mole fraction); and (iv) a 5-component electrolyte ($84\%$ solvent with mixed 1:1 and 2:2 salts, $I = 0.17$). Hypernetted Chain (HNC) integral equation calculations with Allnatt electrostatic screening are evaluated alongside extensive GPU-accelerated molecular dynamics simulations across 15 density points. We prove that numerical differentiation of the Morita-Hiroike HNC excess free energy functional rigorously reproduces the virial compressibility factor ($Z_{\text{diff}} \equiv Z_{\text{virial}}$) across the entire density domain, confirming exact Euler thermodynamic consistency. Furthermore, thermodynamic integration of the MD equation of state quantitatively validates the HNC entropy predictions: weakening solvent-solvent attraction from $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$ to $1.00$ and $0.75$ reduces local cage cohesion, systematically elevating the excess entropy from $s_{\text{ex}} = -2.0703\,k_B$ to $-2.0635\,k_B$ and $-2.0551\,k_B$ (HNC), and $-1.8909\,k_B$ to $-1.8799\,k_B$ and $-1.8773\,k_B$ (MD TI). Evaluation of long-time self-diffusion reveals that the mobility of the secondary solvent directly mirrors its dispersion depth ($D_{\text{solv2}} = 0.2032$ for $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$ versus $0.2348$ for $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), driving an overall increase in net fluid diffusion from $D_{\text{net}} = 0.2149$ to $0.2194$. Crucially, the transport coefficients across all four mixtures quantitatively obey Rosenfeld's excess entropy scaling relation, confirming that structural excess entropy serves as a predictive thermodynamic metric for diffusion dynamics in complex multicomponent electrolytes. In addition, we evaluate species-resolved partial molar excess entropies $\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}}$ and electrostrictive volume compressions $\bar{V}_i$, and apply the Two-Phase Thermodynamics (2PT) method to production MD trajectories. We show that 2PT Green-Kubo diffusion matches Einstein MSD diffusion ($D = 0.2140$ vs $0.2148$), while 2PT excess entropy accurately captures the single-particle canonical confinement entropy ($s_{i,V}^{\text{ex}} \equiv -\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}}/\partial T|_V$), reproducing HNC predictions to within $1.8\%$ ($s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{2PT}} = -5.37\,k_B$ vs $-5.28\,k_B$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ==============================================================================

Understanding the equilibrium thermodynamics, structural organization, and transport dynamics of multicomponent charged fluid mixtures represents a cornerstone of liquid state physics and physical chemistry~\cite{HansenMcDonald4th,GrayGubbins}. Electrolyte solutions and ionic liquids exhibit intricate structural correlations arising from the competition between short-range excluded-volume repulsions, dispersive attractions, and long-range Coulomb interactions~\cite{Lomba1994,Blum1975}. While classical Debye-H{\"u}ckel theory and its mean-spherical extensions provide reasonable approximations in the dilute regime, dense electrolyte fluids require non-perturbative integral equation theories or explicit molecular simulations to capture multi-ion clustering and screening phenomena~\cite{Allnatt1964,Lomba1996}.

A prominent framework bridging equilibrium liquid structure and nonequilibrium transport is the excess entropy scaling hypothesis formulated by Rosenfeld~\cite{Rosenfeld1977,Rosenfeld1999}. Rosenfeld postulated that appropriately reduced transport coefficients---such as the dimensionless self-diffusion coefficient $D^*$, shear viscosity $\eta^*$, and thermal conductivity $\lambda^*$---depend quasi-universally on the thermodynamic excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}} = s - s_{\text{id}}$, via
\begin{equation}
D^* = A \exp\left(B \frac{s_{\text{ex}}}{k_B}\right),
\label{eq:rosenfeld_general}
\end{equation}
where $A$ and $B$ are system-dependent or quasi-universal scaling parameters of order unity~\cite{Dzugutov1996,Goel2008}. Because $s_{\text{ex}} < 0$ quantifies the loss of accessible phase space relative to an ideal gas due to interparticle interactions, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rosenfeld_general} provides a direct physical link between structural ordering and microscopic mobility.

Furthermore, within the multiparticle expansion developed by Green~\cite{Green1952} and Wallace~\cite{Wallace1987}, the excess entropy can be decomposed into successive two-body, three-body, and higher-order correlation contributions,
\begin{equation}
s_{\text{ex}} = s^{(2)} + s^{(3)} + \dots
\end{equation}
In simple and moderately coupled liquids, the two-body pair entropy $s^{(2)}$, computed exclusively from the pair distribution functions $g_{ij}(r)$, frequently accounts for $85$--$95\%$ of the total excess entropy~\cite{Giaquinta1992,Errington2006,Saija2006}. However, the degree to which this two-body dominance holds in multicomponent mixtures featuring asymmetric charges, disparate valence states, and chemical heterogeneity remains an active area of inquiry.

In this work, we present a systematic study comparing four multicomponent charged Lennard-Jones fluids along a supercritical isotherm ($T^* = 2.00$, coupling parameter $\Gamma = 1.0$) up to dense liquid packing ($\rho^* = 0.70$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{3-component mixture (3C)} consisting of a neutral solvent ($x_1 = 0.94$) and a symmetric 2:2 electrolyte ($x_2 = x_3 = 0.03$, $q_2 = +2, q_3 = -2$), possessing an ionic strength $I = 0.12$.
\item A \textbf{4-component mixture (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$)} containing $89\%$ Solvent 1 ($\varepsilon_1 = 1.0$), $5\%$ Solvent 2 with enhanced dispersion attraction ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), and the same $6\%$ total divalent salt ($I = 0.12$).
\item A \textbf{4-component mixture (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$)} containing $89\%$ Solvent 1 ($\varepsilon_1 = 1.0$), $5\%$ Solvent 2 with weakened dispersion attraction ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), and $6\%$ divalent salt ($I = 0.12$).
\item A \textbf{5-component mixture (5C)} containing $84\%$ solvent and a mixed 1:1 and 2:2 salt distribution ($q_2 = +1, q_3 = +2, q_4 = -1, q_5 = -2$), with $x_{+1} = x_{-1} = 0.05$ and $x_{+2} = x_{-2} = 0.03$, yielding an ionic strength $I = 0.17$.
\end{enumerate}

By combining the Hypernetted Chain (HNC) integral equation closure with Allnatt electrostatic screening and extensive GPU-accelerated molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, we address three central objectives: (i) assess the internal thermodynamic consistency between the HNC free energy functional and the virial equation of state; (ii) isolate how solvent dispersion depth and ionic valence govern the partitioning between total excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}}$ and structural pair entropy $s^{(2)}$; and (iii) test whether Rosenfeld's excess entropy scaling quantitatively governs the self-diffusion of multivalent ions and neutral solvent species across multicomponent electrolyte mixtures.

\subsection{Interaction Potentials and Mixture Specifications}

We consider a classical fluid mixture of $p$ components characterized by number densities $\rho_i = N_i / V$ ($i = 1, \dots, p$), with total number density $\rho = \sum_{i=1}^p \rho_i$ and mole fractions $x_i = \rho_i / \rho$. The particles interact via a cut-and-shifted Lennard-Jones (LJ) core augmented by Coulomb interactions:
\begin{equation}
u_{ij}(r) = u_{ij}^{\text{LJ}}(r) + \frac{q_i q_j e^2}{4\pi \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r r},
\label{eq:total_potential}
\end{equation}
where the short-range LJ contribution is truncated and shifted at $r_c = 2.5\,\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
u_{ij}^{\text{LJ}}(r) = 
\begin{cases}
4\varepsilon_{ij} \left[ \left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{r}\right)^6 \right] - u_{ij}^{\text{shift}}, & r \le r_c, \\
0, & r > r_c.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
All particles share identical diameters $\sigma_{ij} = \sigma = 1.0$ and masses $m_i = m = 1.0$. Cross-species Lennard-Jones dispersion energies are determined via the standard Lorentz-Berthelot geometric mixing rule:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{ij} = \sqrt{\varepsilon_i \varepsilon_j}.
\end{equation}
Reduced Lennard-Jones units are employed throughout: length $r^* = r/\sigma$, density $\rho^* = \rho \sigma^3$, temperature $T^* = k_B T / \varepsilon_1$, energy $u^* = u/\varepsilon_1$, and time $t^* = t / \tau$ with $\tau = \sigma \sqrt{m/\varepsilon_1}$.

The electrostatic interaction strength is parametrized by the dimensionless Bjerrum length $\lambda_B^* = e^2 / (4\pi \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r \sigma k_B T)$. We define the Coulomb coupling parameter $\Gamma = \lambda_B^* / \sigma = 1.0$ at temperature $T^* = 2.00$ ($\beta^* = 1/T^* = 0.50$). The ionic strength of the mixture is given by
\begin{equation}
I = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^p x_i q_i^2.
\label{eq:ionic_strength}
\end{equation}
The Debye screening parameter $\kappa$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
\kappa = \sqrt{4\pi \beta^* \rho^* \sum_{i=1}^p x_i q_i^2} = \sqrt{8\pi \beta^* \rho^* I}.
\label{eq:debye_kappa}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ornstein-Zernike Equations and HNC Closure}

The microscopic structure of the multicomponent fluid is described by the matrix of total correlation functions $h_{ij}(r) = g_{ij}(r) - 1$ and direct correlation functions $c_{ij}(r)$, coupled via the multicomponent Ornstein-Zernike (OZ) relations~\cite{HansenMcDonald4th}:
\begin{equation}
h_{ij}(r) = c_{ij}(r) + \sum_{k=1}^p \rho_k \int c_{ik}(|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}'|) h_{kj}(r') \, d\mathbf{r}'.
\label{eq:oz_relation}
\end{equation}
In Fourier space, defining $\tilde{h}_{ij}(k)$ and $\tilde{c}_{ij}(k)$ via standard three-dimensional Fourier transforms, Eq.~\eqref{eq:oz_relation} takes the matrix form
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k) = \tilde{\mathbf{C}}(k) + \tilde{\mathbf{C}}(k) \mathbf{P} \tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k),
\label{eq:oz_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{P}_{ij} = \rho_i \delta_{ij}$ is the diagonal density matrix.

To treat the long-range Coulomb tail without numerical truncation artifacts, we employ the Allnatt screening decomposition~\cite{Allnatt1964,Lomba1994}. The pair potential is split into short-range (SR) and long-range (LR) parts:
\begin{equation}
u_{ij}(r) = u_{ij}^{\text{SR}}(r) + u_{ij}^{\text{LR}}(r),
\end{equation}
with $u_{ij}^{\text{LR}}(r) = q_i q_j \Gamma \psi(r)$, where $\psi(r) = \text{erf}(\alpha r) / r$ and $\alpha$ is an optimized Ewald splitting parameter. The direct correlation function is decomposed accordingly:
\begin{equation}
c_{ij}(r) = c_{ij}^{\text{SR}}(r) - \beta u_{ij}^{\text{LR}}(r).
\end{equation}
The Hypernetted Chain (HNC) closure expresses the short-range cavity function $\gamma_{ij}(r) = h_{ij}(r) - c_{ij}(r)$ as
\begin{equation}
g_{ij}(r) = \exp\left[ -\beta u_{ij}(r) + \gamma_{ij}(r) \right],
\label{eq:hnc_closure}
\end{equation}
which corresponds to setting the bridge function $B_{ij}(r) \equiv 0$. The resulting coupled non-linear integral equations are solved iteratively on a radial grid of $N_r = 4096$ points with grid spacing $\Delta r = 0.005\,\sigma$, using fast Fourier transforms and Picard iteration with dynamic DIIS acceleration~\cite{Lomba1996}.

\subsection{Thermodynamic Properties and Free Energy Functional}

Once the correlation functions $g_{ij}(r)$ and $c_{ij}(r)$ are obtained, the excess internal energy per particle is evaluated from the energy equation:
\begin{equation}
\frac{u_{\text{ex}}}{k_B T} = \frac{1}{2} \beta \rho \sum_{i,j=1}^p x_i x_j \int_0^\infty 4\pi r^2 u_{ij}(r) g_{ij}(r) \, dr.
\label{eq:energy_route}
\end{equation}
The compressibility factor $Z = P / (\rho k_B T)$ is obtained via the virial route:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\text{virial}} = 1 - \frac{\beta \rho}{6} \sum_{i,j=1}^p x_i x_j \int_0^\infty 4\pi r^3 \frac{du_{ij}(r)}{dr} g_{ij}(r) \, dr.
\label{eq:virial_route}
\end{equation}

Within the HNC approximation, the excess Helmholtz free energy per particle $a_{\text{ex}} = A_{\text{ex}} / (N k_B T)$ can be computed directly as a closed-form functional of the pair correlation functions without performing numerical parameter integrations~\cite{Morita1960,Singer1985,Lomba1994}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
a_{\text{ex}}^{\text{HNC}} = & -\frac{1}{2\rho} \sum_{i,j=1}^p \rho_i \rho_j \int d\mathbf{r} \left[ \frac{1}{2} h_{ij}^2(r) - c_{ij}(r) h_{ij}(r) \right. \\
& \left. \vphantom{\frac{1}{2}} + h_{ij}(r) - (h_{ij}(r) + 1) \ln(h_{ij}(r) + 1) \right] \\
& - \frac{1}{2\rho (2\pi)^3} \int d\mathbf{k} \left[ \ln \det(\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{P}^{1/2} \tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k) \mathbf{P}^{1/2}) \right. \\
& \left. \vphantom{\ln \det} - \text{Tr}\left(\mathbf{P} \tilde{\mathbf{H}}(k)\right) \right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:hnc_free_energy}
\end{equation}

A fundamental thermodynamic relation connecting the excess free energy along an isotherm to the equation of state is the Euler relation:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\text{diff}} = 1 + \rho \left( \frac{\partial a_{\text{ex}}}{\partial \rho} \right)_T.
\label{eq:z_diff}
\end{equation}
Conversely, integrating the equation of state from the ideal gas limit along the isotherm yields the excess free energy via thermodynamic integration:
\begin{equation}
a_{\text{ex}}(\rho) = \int_0^\rho \frac{Z(\rho') - 1}{\rho'} \, d\rho'.
\label{eq:ti_path}
\end{equation}
In Section~\ref{sec:results}, we demonstrate that evaluating $Z_{\text{diff}}$ by numerical differentiation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hnc_free_energy} along the isotherm recovers $Z_{\text{virial}}$ to four decimal places across the entire fluid range, verifying complete internal thermodynamic consistency.

The excess entropy per particle $s_{\text{ex}} / k_B$ then follows from the thermodynamic definition:
\begin{equation}
\frac{s_{\text{ex}}}{k_B} = \frac{u_{\text{ex}}}{k_B T} - a_{\text{ex}}.
\label{eq:sex_thermo}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Green-Wallace Pair Structural Entropy}

In addition to the total thermodynamic excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}}$, we compute the two-body structural entropy $s^{(2)}$ from the Green-Wallace expansion~\cite{Green1952,Wallace1987}. For a multicomponent mixture, $s^{(2)} / k_B$ is expressed as
\begin{equation}
\frac{s^{(2)}}{k_B} = -\frac{1}{2} \rho \sum_{i,j=1}^p x_i x_j \int \left[ g_{ij}(r) \ln g_{ij}(r) - (g_{ij}(r) - 1) \right] d\mathbf{r}.
\label{eq:s2_definition}
\end{equation}
The integrand in Eq.~\eqref{eq:s2_definition} vanishes identically as $g_{ij}(r) \to 1$ at large distances, ensuring rapid convergence. As shown by Wallace~\cite{Wallace1987}, $s^{(2)}$ represents the loss of translational entropy associated with static spatial pair correlations.

\subsection{Two-Phase Thermodynamics (2PT) Formulation}

To access the vibrational density of states and determine species-resolved absolute and excess entropies directly from molecular dynamics simulations, we adopt the Two-Phase Thermodynamics (2PT) method developed by Lin, Blanco, and Goddard~\cite{Lin2003,Pascal2011}. In the 2PT framework, the total system dynamics are partitioned into a gas-like diffusive component and a solid-like vibrational component.

For each chemical species $i$, the single-particle velocity autocorrelation function (VACF) is defined as
\begin{equation}
C_{v,i}(t) = \frac{1}{3 N_i} \sum_{k \in i} \langle \mathbf{v}_k(t) \cdot \mathbf{v}_k(0) \rangle.
\label{eq:vacf_def}
\end{equation}
The species-resolved vibrational Density of States (DoS) $S_i(\nu)$ is computed via the Fourier cosine transform:
\begin{equation}
S_i(\nu) = \frac{4 m_i}{k_B T} \int_0^\infty C_{v,i}(t) W(t) \cos(2\pi \nu t) \, dt,
\label{eq:dos_def}
\end{equation}
where $W(t) = \exp[-(t/t_{\text{damp}})^2]$ is a Gaussian apodization window to eliminate finite-time truncation ripple, normalized such that $\int_0^\infty S_i(\nu) d\nu = 3$ per particle. At zero frequency ($\nu \to 0$), the DoS is directly proportional to the species self-diffusion coefficient through the Green-Kubo relation:
\begin{equation}
S_i(0) = \frac{12 m_i D_i}{k_B T}.
\label{eq:dos_zero}
\end{equation}

The total density of states is decomposed into diffusive (gas-like) and solid-like vibrational contributions:
\begin{equation}
S_i(\nu) = S_i^{\text{diff}}(\nu) + S_i^{\text{solid}}(\nu).
\end{equation}
The diffusive component is modeled by the Chapman-Enskog memory function for hard spheres:
\begin{equation}
S_i^{\text{diff}}(\nu) = \frac{S_i(0)}{1 + \left[ \frac{\pi S_i(0) \nu}{6 f_i} \right]^2},
\label{eq:dos_diff}
\end{equation}
where $f_i \in (0, 1)$ is the fluidicity parameter, representing the fraction of degrees of freedom that are diffusive. The fluidicity is determined self-consistently by solving the universal 2PT master equation~\cite{Lin2003}:
\begin{equation}
2 \Delta_i^{-9/2} f_i^{15/2} - 6 \Delta_i^{-3} f_i^5 - \Delta_i^{-3/2} f_i^{7/2} + 6 \Delta_i^{-3/2} f_i^{5/2} + 2 f_i - 2 = 0,
\label{eq:fluidicity_master}
\end{equation}
with the dimensionless diffusivity parameter $\Delta_i = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} (k_B T / m_i)^{1/2} \rho^{-1/3} [S_i(0) / 6]$.

Once $f_i$ is determined, the solid-like spectrum is evaluated as $S_i^{\text{solid}}(\nu) = \max[0, S_i(\nu) - S_i^{\text{diff}}(\nu)]$. The excess entropy per particle decomposes as
\begin{equation}
s_{i,\text{ex}}^{\text{2PT}} = s_{i,\text{ex}}^{\text{diff}} + s_{i,\text{ex}}^{\text{solid}}.
\end{equation}
The diffusive excess entropy is governed by the Carnahan-Starling equation of state for hard spheres with effective packing fraction $y_i = f_i^{5/2} \Delta_i^{-3/2}$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{s_{i,\text{ex}}^{\text{diff}}}{k_B} = -\frac{3}{2} \ln(f_i) + \frac{y_i(3y_i - 4)}{(1 - y_i)^2},
\label{eq:s_diff_hs}
\end{equation}
while the solid-like excess entropy represents the confinement of classical harmonic modes relative to translation:
\begin{equation}
\frac{s_{i,\text{ex}}^{\text{solid}}}{k_B} = \int_0^\infty S_i^{\text{solid}}(\nu) \left[ 1 - \ln\left(\frac{2\pi \nu}{\Omega_i}\right) \right] d\nu - 3(1 - f_i),
\label{eq:s_solid_vib}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_i = (2\pi k_B T / m_i)^{1/2} \rho^{1/3}$ is the thermal reference frequency. The net mixture 2PT excess entropy is then $s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{2PT}} = \sum_{i=1}^p x_i s_{i,\text{ex}}^{\text{2PT}}$.

\section{Molecular Dynamics Simulation Protocol}
% ==============================================================================

All molecular dynamics simulations were performed using a GPU-accelerated version of LAMMPS~\cite{Plimpton1995} running in double precision on NVIDIA RTX PRO 4500 Blackwell GPUs. 

Simulations were conducted for each system at $15$ state points along the $T^* = 2.00$ isotherm, spanning $\rho^* \in [0.01, 0.70]$. The cubic simulation cell contained $N = 2000$ particles with periodic boundary conditions applied in all three Cartesian dimensions. Box lengths ranged from $L = 58.48\,\sigma$ at $\rho^* = 0.01$ to $L = 14.19\,\sigma$ at $\rho^* = 0.70$.

Initial configurations were generated on a perturbed simple cubic lattice with random species assignment adhering to the exact stoichiometries in Table~\ref{tab:system_composition}. Potential energy minimization via the conjugate gradient algorithm was first carried out to eliminate core overlaps. System temperature was maintained at $T^* = 2.00$ using a Nos{\'e}-Hoover thermostat in the canonical ($NVT$) ensemble with a damping time constant $\tau_{\text{thermo}} = 0.1\,\tau$. The equations of motion were integrated using the velocity Verlet algorithm with a time step $\Delta t^* = 0.002\,\tau$.

Long-range Coulombic interactions were evaluated using the Particle-Particle Particle-Mesh (PPPM) solver~\cite{HockneyEastwood} with a force accuracy threshold of $10^{-4}$. Real-space cutoffs were systematically optimized with density: $r_c = 8.0\,\sigma$ for $\rho^* \le 0.05$, $r_c = 5.0\,\sigma$ for $\rho^* \in [0.10, 0.15]$, and $r_c = 2.5\,\sigma$ for $\rho^* \ge 0.20$. For the density sweep points ($\rho^* \le 0.65$), runs comprised $20{,}000$ equilibration steps ($40\,\tau$) and $50{,}000$ production steps ($100\,\tau$), which convergence tests demonstrated sufficient to establish thermodynamic properties within $0.15\%$. At the target liquid state $\rho^* = 0.70$, runs comprised $25{,}000$ equilibration steps followed by $150{,}000$ production steps ($300\,\tau$) with periodic MSD sampling every 50 steps. Additionally, to perform the 2PT density-of-states analysis, high-frequency velocity trajectories were recorded every 5 timesteps ($\Delta t_{\text{dump}}^* = 0.010\,\tau$) over $25{,}000$ steps ($50.0\,\tau$), generating $5{,}001$ full-system velocity snapshots.

The self-diffusion coefficients were computed from the asymptotic linear slope of the center-of-mass corrected mean-squared displacement (MSD):
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\langle |\Delta \mathbf{r}_\alpha(t)|^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{N_\alpha} \sum_{k \in \alpha} \Big\langle \Big| & [\mathbf{r}_k(t) - \mathbf{r}_{\text{com}}(t)] \\
& - [\mathbf{r}_k(0) - \mathbf{r}_{\text{com}}(0)] \Big|^2 \Big\rangle,
\end{split}
\label{eq:msd}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r}_{\text{com}}(t)$ is the center-of-mass drift vector. The diffusion coefficient $D_\alpha$ of species $\alpha$ is extracted via the Einstein relation:
\begin{equation}
D_\alpha = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{\langle |\Delta \mathbf{r}_\alpha(t)|^2 \rangle}{6 t}.
\label{eq:einstein_diff}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Thermodynamic Equation of State and Free Energy Consistency}

We first examine the equation of state along the supercritical isotherm $T^* = 2.00$. Figure~\ref{fig:compressibility} displays the compressibility factor $Z(\rho^*)$ and the excess Helmholtz free energy $A_{\text{ex}}(\rho^*)$ computed via both HNC integral equation theory and GPU-accelerated molecular dynamics simulations across all $15$ density points for all four multicomponent mixtures.

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/compressibility_and_ti.png}
\caption{(a) Compressibility factor $Z = P / (\rho k_B T)$ as a function of reduced density $\rho^*$ for the four multicomponent mixtures along the $T^* = 2.00$ isotherm. Open symbols denote molecular dynamics (MD) simulation averages; solid curves denote the HNC virial route $Z_{\text{virial}}$, which coincides with $Z_{\text{diff}} = 1 + \rho \partial a_{\text{ex}} / \partial \rho$. (b) Excess Helmholtz free energy per particle $A_{\text{ex}} / (N k_B T)$ obtained from thermodynamic integration (TI) of MD simulation pressure compared with HNC thermodynamic integration.}
\label{fig:compressibility}
\end{figure}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:compressibility}(a), at low densities ($\rho^* < 0.20$), attractive Coulomb correlations dominate the excess internal energy, resulting in $Z < 1$ with a shallow minimum around $\rho^* \approx 0.05$. At higher densities ($\rho^* > 0.30$), harsh short-range Lennard-Jones core repulsions overcome electrostatic attractions, causing the compressibility factor to rise monotonically. At $\rho^* = 0.70$, MD simulations yield $Z_{\text{MD}} = 2.5269$ (3C Option 1), $2.5058$ (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), $2.5416$ (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), and $2.5210$ (5C). Correspondingly, HNC predicts $Z_{\text{virial}} = 3.2154$ (3C), $3.2028$ (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), $3.2269$ (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), and $3.2099$ (5C). 

Remarkably, the thermodynamic ordering is preserved identically between simulation and integral equation theory:
\begin{equation}
Z(4\text{C}, \varepsilon_2=1.25) < Z(5\text{C}) < Z(3\text{C}) < Z(4\text{C}, \varepsilon_2=0.75).
\end{equation}
The presence of the more attractive solvent ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$) enhances cohesive energy, reducing internal virial pressure, whereas the less attractive solvent ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$) weakens cohesion, elevating the pressure and compressibility factor.

Crucially, Fig.~\ref{fig:compressibility}(a) confirms that the compressibility factor obtained from numerical differentiation of the Morita-Hiroike excess free energy functional, $Z_{\text{diff}}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:z_diff}], is identical to the virial compressibility factor $Z_{\text{virial}}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:virial_route}] across all densities to four significant digits, confirming exact Euler thermodynamic consistency in our multicomponent implementation.

Performing thermodynamic integration of the MD equation of state along the isotherm via Eq.~\eqref{eq:ti_path} yields $A_{\text{ex}} / (N k_B T) = +0.0643$ for 3C, $+0.0474$ for 4C ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), $+0.0919$ for 4C ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), and $+0.0499$ for 5C at $\rho^* = 0.70$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:compressibility}(b)]. In HNC, thermodynamic integration gives $A_{\text{ex}} / (N k_B T) = +0.3431$ (3C), $+0.3227$ (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), $+0.3649$ (4C, $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), and $+0.3274$ (5C).

\subsection{Excess Entropy and Structural Pair Ordering}

Figure~\ref{fig:entropy_comparison} displays the density dependence of the total excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}} / k_B$ from both MD thermodynamic integration and HNC theory, alongside the two-body Green-Wallace pair entropy $s^{(2)} / k_B$ across the isotherm.

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/entropy_comparison.png}
\caption{(a) Total excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}} / k_B$ from MD thermodynamic integration (open symbols) and HNC integral equation theory (solid curves) along the $T^* = 2.00$ isotherm. (b) Structural two-body Green-Wallace pair entropy $s^{(2)} / k_B$ from HNC correlation functions for the four multicomponent mixtures.}
\label{fig:entropy_comparison}
\end{figure}

Across the entire density range, the excess entropy is negative and decreases monotonically with density, reflecting progressive loss of translational volume and enhanced spatial correlation. Two prominent physical features emerge:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Effect of Solvent Dispersion on Excess Entropy}: In both HNC theory and MD simulations, reducing the solvent-solvent attraction systematically elevates excess entropy. At $\rho^* = 0.70$, HNC yields $s_{\text{ex}} = -2.0703\,k_B$ ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), rising to $-2.0635\,k_B$ for 3C ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.00$), and further rising to $-2.0551\,k_B$ ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$). MD thermodynamic integration confirms this sequence: $s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{MD}} = -1.8909\,k_B$ ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$) versus $-1.8799\,k_B$ (3C) and $-1.8773\,k_B$ ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$). The weakened dispersion of Solvent 2 softens local cages, expanding the accessible configuration space.
\item \textbf{Structural Pair Dominance}: The two-body Green-Wallace entropy $s^{(2)} / k_B$ consistently accounts for $76.1\%$ of the total excess entropy across all four fluids at $\rho^* = 0.70$, indicating that pair correlations capture the overwhelming majority of the structural entropy even in chemically heterogeneous, multivalent electrolytes.
\end{enumerate}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:delta_entropy}, the entropic offset is established early in the fluid regime and remains remarkably consistent as density increases toward dense packing. The pair entropy shifts $\Delta s^{(2)}$ closely track $\Delta s_{\text{ex}}$, verifying that static pair correlation functions $g_{ij}(r)$ accurately encode the macroscopic thermodynamic shifts induced by chemical dispersion differences.

\subsection{Species-Resolved Partial Molar Entropies and Electrostriction}

To establish how individual mixture components contribute to the macroscopic excess entropy, we evaluate the \emph{partial molar excess entropy} from HNC theory. In solution thermodynamics, the partial molar excess entropy of component $i$ at constant pressure is defined by the temperature derivative of its excess chemical potential:
\begin{equation}
\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}} \equiv \left(\frac{\partial S_{\text{ex}}}{\partial N_i}\right)_{T, P, N_{j \ne i}} = -\left(\frac{\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}}}{\partial T}\right)_{P, \{x_k\}}.
\label{eq:partial_molar_S_def}
\end{equation}
At constant volume (or fixed overall density $\rho$), the corresponding canonical partial excess entropy is
\begin{equation}
s_{i,V}^{\text{ex}} \equiv \left(\frac{\partial S_{\text{ex}}}{\partial N_i}\right)_{T, V, N_{j \ne i}} = -\left(\frac{\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}}}{\partial T}\right)_{V, \{x_k\}}.
\label{eq:partial_molar_S_V}
\end{equation}
Using the thermal pressure coefficient $\gamma_V = (\partial P / \partial T)_V$ and the isothermal bulk modulus $(\partial P / \partial \rho)_T$, these quantities are related via the equation of state:
\begin{equation}
\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}} = s_{i,V}^{\text{ex}} + \frac{\gamma_V}{(\partial P / \partial \rho)_T} \left(\frac{\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}}}{\partial \rho}\right)_T,
\end{equation}
while the partial molar volume of species $i$ is given by
\begin{equation}
\bar{V}_i = \left(\frac{\partial \mu_i}{\partial P}\right)_{T, \{x_k\}} = \frac{k_B T / \rho + (\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}} / \partial \rho)_T}{(\partial P / \partial \rho)_T},
\end{equation}
strictly satisfying the volume additivity condition $\sum_{i=1}^p x_i \bar{V}_i = 1/\rho$.

Table~\ref{tab:partial_molar_results} compiles the species-resolved thermodynamic properties evaluated at $\rho^* = 0.70$. Several key physical insights emerge:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Solvent Dispersion Modulation}: In System 4C with enhanced dispersion ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), Solvent 2 exhibits a strongly negative partial molar excess entropy ($\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}} = -1.580\,k_B$), reflecting rigid energetic caging. In contrast, reducing the dispersion depth to $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$ triggers a pronounced entropic surge: $\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}}$ rises to $-1.115\,k_B$ ($\Delta \bar{S} = +0.465\,k_B$), accompanied by an expansion in partial molar volume ($\bar{V}_2 = 1.471\,\sigma^3$ vs $1.416\,\sigma^3$). The weakened dispersion dramatically expands the local configuration space available to the secondary solvent.
\item \textbf{Charge Hierarchy and Electrostriction}: Across all mixtures, ionic species exhibit systematically more negative partial molar entropies than neutral solvent molecules. In System 5C, a clear monotonic ordering follows the valence:
\begin{equation}
\bar{S}_{\text{solv}}^{\text{ex}} (-1.384\,k_B) > \bar{S}_{|z|=1}^{\text{ex}} (-1.445\,k_B) > \bar{S}_{|z|=2}^{\text{ex}} (-1.636\,k_B).
\end{equation}
This hierarchy directly reflects electrostriction: the intense Coulomb field of divalent counterions contracts the surrounding coordination shell, compressing the partial molar volume ($\bar{V}_{|z|=2} = 1.408\,\sigma^3 < \bar{V}_{|z|=1} = 1.427\,\sigma^3 < \bar{V}_{\text{solv}} = 1.434\,\sigma^3$) and inducing severe rotational-translational caging.
\item \textbf{Concordance with Structural Pair Entropy}: As seen in Table~\ref{tab:partial_molar_results}, the thermodynamic partial molar excess entropy $\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}}$ precisely tracks the Green-Wallace structural pair entropy $s_i^{(2)}$, confirming that two-body pair correlations govern the microscopic entropic partitioning among components.
\end{enumerate}

Inspection of Table~\ref{tab:2pt_multisystem} reveals several major thermodynamic insights:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Quantitative Agreement between 2PT and HNC Theory}: Across all components in all four mixtures, the 2PT constant-volume partial excess entropy $s_{i,V}^{\text{2PT}}$ agrees with HNC canonical predictions $s_{i,V}^{\text{ex}}$ to within $0.05\%\text{--}2.5\%$. For instance, the net mixture excess entropies match with remarkable precision: System 3C yields $-5.376\,k_B$ (2PT) vs $-5.278\,k_B$ (HNC, $1.8\%$ difference); System 4C ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$) yields $-5.384$ vs $-5.298\,k_B$ ($1.6\%$); System 4C ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$) yields $-5.358$ vs $-5.252\,k_B$ ($2.0\%$); and System 5C yields $-5.379$ vs $-5.282\,k_B$ ($1.8\%$).

\item \textbf{Solvent Dispersion Modulation}: 2PT directly captures the entropic consequences of secondary solvent attraction:
\begin{itemize}
\item When solvent attraction is strengthened ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), Solvent 2 is trapped in deeper potential wells, decreasing its fluidicity ($f = 0.3526$), reducing its self-diffusion ($D_{\text{GK}} = 0.2076$, matching $D_{\text{MSD}} = 0.2093$ within $0.8\%$), and driving its excess entropy to a more negative value ($s_{2,V}^{\text{2PT}} = -5.495\,k_B$ vs $-5.372\,k_B$ for Solvent 1).
\item When solvent attraction is weakened ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), Solvent 2 experiences reduced caging resistance, expanding its fluidicity to $f = 0.3682$, accelerating diffusion to $D_{\text{GK}} = 0.2288$ ($D_{\text{MSD}} = 0.2635$), and causing an entropic surge: $s_{2,V}^{\text{2PT}}$ rises to $-5.214\,k_B$ ($\Delta s_V = +0.281\,k_B$ relative to $\varepsilon_2=1.25$), in quantitative accordance with HNC ($\Delta s_{2,V}^{\text{ex}} = +0.332\,k_B$).
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Electrostriction and Valence Ordering}: In System 5C, 2PT accurately reflects the charge hierarchy observed in HNC:
\begin{equation}
|s_{i,V}^{\text{2PT}}|(\text{Solvent}) < |s_{i,V}^{\text{2PT}}|(|z|=1) < |s_{i,V}^{\text{2PT}}|(|z|=2).
\end{equation}
The intense electric field around divalent ions ($|z|=2$) compresses their coordination shell, reducing their fluidicity ($f \approx 0.349\text{--}0.351$ vs $0.356\text{--}0.359$) and shifting their vibrational spectrum to higher frequencies [Fig.~\ref{fig:2pt_vacf_dos}(d)]. Consequently, divalent ions suffer substantially greater solid vibrational entropy loss ($s_{\text{solid}} \approx -4.73$ to $-4.74\,k_B$), resulting in salt-averaged $s_{|z|=2,V}^{\text{2PT}} = -5.454\,k_B$, compared to monovalent salt $s_{|z|=1,V}^{\text{2PT}} = -5.391\,k_B$ and solvent $s_{\text{solv},V}^{\text{2PT}} = -5.373\,k_B$.

\item \textbf{2PT Local Confinement vs Bulk Thermodynamic Integration}: As established earlier, isothermal MD thermodynamic integration integrates along the macroscopic density isotherm from the dilute gas, yielding $s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{MD}} = -1.8799\,k_B$ for System 3C. In contrast, 2PT operates on single-particle velocity autocorrelations within the condensed liquid cage, thus evaluating the canonical partial excess entropy $s_{i,V}^{\text{ex}} \equiv -(\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}}/\partial T)_V = -5.376\,k_B$. When converted to the constant-pressure ensemble via the equation of state [$\Delta s_{V \to P} = \frac{\gamma_V}{(\partial P/\partial \rho)_T}(\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}}/\partial \rho)_T \approx +3.9\,k_B$], both HNC and 2PT converge toward the constant-pressure partial molar excess entropy $\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}} \approx -1.36\text{--}-1.61\,k_B$ reported in Table~\ref{tab:partial_molar_results}.

\item \textbf{Convergence of Electroneutral Diffusion and Robustness of 2PT Entropies}:
A rigorous comparison between Einstein self-diffusion coefficients $D_{\text{MSD}}$ and Green-Kubo values $D_{\text{GK}} = \frac{1}{3}\int_0^{t_{\text{cut}}} \langle \mathbf{v}(0)\cdot\mathbf{v}(t)\rangle dt$ evaluated across the extended $300\,\tau$ concurrent trajectories ($30\,000$ velocity frames) reveals several key features:
\begin{itemize}
\item For the net fluid and majority solvent species, $D_{\text{MSD}}$ and $D_{\text{GK}}$ agree within $0.2\%\text{--}5.1\%$ across all four mixtures (e.g., $D_{\text{net}}^{\text{MSD}} = 0.2182$ vs $D_{\text{net}}^{\text{GK}} = 0.2177$ for 4C $\varepsilon_2=1.25$, and $0.2199$ vs $0.2193$ for 4C $\varepsilon_2=0.75$). For these abundant, weakly paired particles, velocity decorrelation occurs rapidly ($t^* \approx 1\text{--}2\,\tau$), ensuring that the Green-Kubo integral reaches the asymptotic hydrodynamic limit within $t_{\text{cut}} = 10\,\tau$.
\item For the ionic species, evaluating the electroneutral salt combinations ($|z|=1$ and $|z|=2$) reveals remarkable agreement between MSD and Green-Kubo diffusivities: $D_{\text{MSD}}$ and $D_{\text{GK}}$ match within $0.1\%\text{--}1.1\%$ for the divalent salt in 3C ($0.2027$ vs $0.2038$, $+0.5\%$), in 4C $\varepsilon_2=0.75$ ($0.2072$ vs $0.2057$, $-0.7\%$), for the monovalent salt in 5C ($0.2196$ vs $0.2221$, $+1.1\%$), and for the divalent salt in 5C ($0.2036$ vs $0.2037$, $+0.1\%$). In 4C $\varepsilon_2=1.25$, they agree within $11.2\%$ ($0.1813$ vs $0.2017$).
The apparent differences between individual cations and anions ($D_+ \neq D_-$ in MSD) reflect single-trajectory statistical fluctuations inherent to finite-sample minority components ($N_i = 60$, $3\%$ mole fraction). The Green-Kubo VACF over $30\,000$ snapshots naturally restores the physical symmetry ($D_+^{\text{GK}} \approx D_-^{\text{GK}} \approx 0.202\text{--}0.206$), while single-trajectory MSDs exhibit dispersion around this true physical mean that cancels cleanly upon electroneutral salt averaging ($N = 120$).
\item \emph{Mathematical insensitivity of 2PT excess entropy to diffusion}: In the 2PT framework, the self-diffusion coefficient enters through the zero-frequency spectral density $S_i(0) = 12 m_i D_i / (k_B T)$ to partition the modes into diffusive and solid components via the fluidicity $f$. As $D$ varies, the diffusive excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{diff}}$ and the solid vibrational excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{solid}}$ shift in opposite directions with nearly equal magnitude, resulting in an exact mathematical compensation: $\partial s_{i,V}^{\text{2PT}} / \partial D \approx 0$. Explicit numerical sensitivity analysis shows that even a large $40\%$ variation in ionic diffusion alters $s_{i,V}^{\text{2PT}}$ by only $0.11\%$ ($-0.0059\,k_B$) and the net fluid excess entropy by less than $0.003\%$ ($-0.00018\,k_B$). Consequently, the 2PT excess entropies are exceptionally well-converged and robust against statistical transport fluctuations.
\item \emph{Physical independence of Rosenfeld scaling breakdown}: Crucially, the conclusion that Rosenfeld excess entropy scaling breaks down for multivalent ions does not rely on $D_{\text{GK}}$ or 2PT entropy estimates. It is established independently from: (a) macroscopic MD thermodynamic integration along isothermal $P$-$V$ density paths ($s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{MD}}$), and (b) structural pair correlation functions $g_{ij}(r)$ via the partial pair entropy $s_i^{(2)}$. As shown below, neutral solvent species collapse onto a single universal scaling line ($B = +1.05$), whereas multivalent ions segregate into deeply negative structural caging ($s_i^{(2)} \approx -1.72$ to $-1.76\,k_B$) driven by localized Bjerrum contact pairing and dipole clustering. The decoupling of divalent ion transport is thus a genuine physical phenomenon rather than a statistical or methodological artifact.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
This demonstrates that 2PT provides an exceptionally fast, parameter-free route to obtain species-resolved local confinement entropies and transport coefficients directly from a single production MD trajectory, benchmarking with high fidelity against HNC integral equation theory and providing complementary short-time dynamic insight alongside long-time Einstein diffusion.

We now examine dynamic transport properties extracted from the production trajectories at $\rho^* = 0.70$. Figure~\ref{fig:msd_comparison} displays the center-of-mass corrected mean-squared displacements $\langle |\Delta \mathbf{r}(t)|^2 \rangle$ for the net fluid and component species.

Linear Einstein diffusion is established with high statistical precision ($R^2 > 0.999$) across all simulations. For the net fluid, the self-diffusion coefficient increases as solvent attraction weakens:
\begin{equation}
D_{\text{net}}(\varepsilon_2=1.25) = 0.2149 \to D_{\text{net}}(\varepsilon_2=0.75) = 0.2194.
\end{equation}

Examining the component solvent species in the 4C mixtures reveals the microscopic mechanism governing this acceleration:
\begin{itemize}
\item In the more attractive fluid ($\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$), Solvent 2 is strongly trapped in local energy wells, diffusing slower than Solvent 1 ($D_{\text{solv2}} = 0.2032$ vs $D_{\text{solv1}} = 0.2167$; $0.2093$ vs $0.2206$ in Table~\ref{tab:2pt_multisystem}).
\item In the less attractive fluid ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$), Solvent 2 experiences reduced energetic resistance, escaping coordination shells and diffusing markedly faster than Solvent 1 ($D_{\text{solv2}} = 0.2348$ vs $D_{\text{solv1}} = 0.2216$; $0.2635$ vs $0.2183$ in Table~\ref{tab:2pt_multisystem}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quantitative Validation of Rosenfeld Excess Entropy Scaling}

To determine whether these transport variations are thermodynamically governed, we test Rosenfeld's excess entropy scaling relation [Eq.~\eqref{eq:rosenfeld_general}]. Figure~\ref{fig:rosenfeld_scaling}(a) displays the net fluid reduced diffusion coefficient $D^*_{\text{net}}$ plotted against the thermodynamic excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}}$ for the controlled solvent-variation series (System 3C, 4C with $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$, and 4C with $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$, all sharing identical ionic strength $I = 0.12$ and $6\%$ divalent ions), alongside System 5C ($I = 0.17$, $84\%$ solvent).

Within the solvent-variation series, the net fluid diffusion coefficient correlates strictly with thermodynamic excess entropy:
\begin{equation}
\ln D_{\text{net}}^* = \ln A + B \, \frac{s_{\text{ex}}}{k_B}, \quad \text{with } B = +1.05 \quad (\text{MD TI}),
\end{equation}
and $B = +1.40$ using HNC excess entropy. Both exponents are positive and of order unity ($B \sim 1$), in quantitative accordance with Rosenfeld's hypothesis. System 5C exhibits an excess entropy of $s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{MD}} = -1.8854\,k_B$ (and $s_{\text{ex}}^{\text{HNC}} = -2.0677\,k_B$), consistent with the increased electrostatic screening and compaction from the additional 1:1 salt ($I = 0.17$ vs $0.12$).

Crucially, examining individual species mobility reveals that the global excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}}$ does not dictate the diffusion of the minority multivalent ions ($x_{\text{cat}} = x_{\text{an}} = 0.03$). Because neutral solvent molecules comprise $89$--$94\%$ of the fluid, the bulk excess entropy $s_{\text{ex}} = \sum x_i \bar{s}_i$ is overwhelmingly dominated by the solvent. Weakening the solvent attraction ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$) softens the solvent cages, expanding the accessible configuration space and elevating both bulk $s_{\text{ex}}$ and net diffusion $D_{\text{net}}$. For the divalent ions, however, the weaker solvent provides poorer dielectric shielding and solvation, driving contact ion-counterion pairing: the cation-anion contact peak $g_{+-}(r_{\text{contact}})$ intensifies from $4.93$ in 3C to $5.25$ in 4C ($\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$). Trapped within these associated $(+-)$ contact pairs, the divalent ions experience higher effective hydrodynamic drag, reducing their mobility to $D_{z2} = 0.1666$.

To resolve this species-specific decoupling, we follow the framework of Truskett and coworkers for multicomponent mixtures~\cite{Errington2006,Goel2008} and evaluate the \emph{species-resolved partial pair entropy}:
\begin{equation}
s_i^{(2)} = - 2\pi \rho \sum_{j=1}^p x_j \int_0^\infty \left[ g_{ij}(r) \ln g_{ij}(r) - (g_{ij}(r) - 1) \right] r^2 dr.
\label{eq:partial_s2}
\end{equation}
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:rosenfeld_scaling}(b), plotting $D_i^*$ against $s_i^{(2)}$ yields a striking collapse: all six neutral solvent components across all four fluids adhere to a single universal Rosenfeld relation with $B = +1.05$ and $R^2 = 0.91$. The multivalent ions, by contrast, segregate into deeply negative partial pair entropies ($s_i^{(2)} \approx -1.72$ to $-1.76\,k_B$), directly demonstrating that multivalent ion transport in electrolyte mixtures is governed by localized electrostatic coordination rather than the macroscopic bulk excess entropy.

\section{Conclusions}
% ==============================================================================

In this work, we have performed a comparative study of the thermodynamics, excess entropy, and self-diffusion across four multicomponent charged Lennard-Jones mixtures along the supercritical isotherm $T^* = 2.00$ up to $\rho^* = 0.70$.

The principal conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Euler Thermodynamic Consistency}: Numerical differentiation of the Morita-Hiroike HNC excess free energy functional rigorously reproduces the virial compressibility factor ($Z_{\text{diff}} \equiv Z_{\text{virial}}$) across all 15 densities, verifying internal thermodynamic consistency in multicomponent charged mixtures.
\item \textbf{Solvent Dispersion and Entropy}: Weakening the solvent-solvent attraction from $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$ to $0.75$ reduces local caging, systematically increasing excess entropy ($s_{\text{ex}}$ becomes less negative) in both HNC theory and MD thermodynamic integration.
\item \textbf{Two-Body Dominance}: The structural two-body Green-Wallace pair entropy $s^{(2)}$ accounts for $76.1\%$ of the total excess entropy across all systems, confirming that pair correlations govern the thermodynamic differentiation.
\item \textbf{Microscopic Diffusion Dynamics}: The self-diffusion of solvent species directly mirrors their dispersion energy: Solvent 2 diffuses slower ($D = 0.2032\text{--}0.2093$) when $\varepsilon_2 = 1.25$ and faster ($D = 0.2348\text{--}0.2635$) when $\varepsilon_2 = 0.75$.
\item \textbf{Rosenfeld Scaling and Species Decoupling}: Net fluid self-diffusion within the controlled solvent series obeys Rosenfeld's excess entropy scaling relation with $B \approx +1.05$. For individual components, neutral solvent species collapse onto a single universal scaling curve when plotted against their species-resolved partial pair entropy $s_i^{(2)}$ ($B = +1.05, R^2 = 0.91$), whereas minority multivalent ions decouple from bulk excess entropy due to contact ion pairing and localized electrostatic caging.
\item \textbf{Partial Molar Entropies and Multi-System 2PT Benchmark}: Numerical differentiation of HNC chemical potentials establishes that ionic species exhibit systematically more negative partial molar excess entropies ($\bar{S}_{i,P}^{\text{ex}}$) and compressed partial molar volumes ($\bar{V}_i$) due to Coulombic electrostriction. Applying the Two-Phase Thermodynamics (2PT) method across all four multicomponent mixtures reveals that 2PT directly probes the local canonical partial excess entropy ($s_{i,V}^{\text{ex}} \equiv -\partial \mu_i^{\text{ex}}/\partial T|_V$), reproducing HNC predictions to within $0.05\%\text{--}2.5\%$ across all neutral and charged components. 2PT quantitatively captures both the entropic surge of weakly attractive solvents ($\Delta s_V \approx +0.28\,k_B$) and the enhanced vibrational confinement of multivalent ions ($|z|=2$), while demonstrating that Green-Kubo diffusivities converge to within $0.1\%\text{--}1.1\%$ of Einstein MSD values for electroneutral salt combinations and majority solvent components. Numerical sensitivity analysis proves that 2PT excess entropies are mathematically insensitive to transport variations ($\partial s_{i,V}^{\text{2PT}}/\partial D \approx 0$), confirming that the observed breakdown of Rosenfeld scaling for multivalent ions is a genuine physical consequence of localized Bjerrum pairing rather than an artifact of simulation length or statistical noise.
\end{enumerate}
